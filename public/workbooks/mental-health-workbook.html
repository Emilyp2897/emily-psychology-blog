How to use this

Nothing in here needs doing in order, and nothing needs finishing. Print the pages you will use and leave the rest.

  • 1 Start with one page. The Thought Log or the Anxiety Tracker are the usual starting points. Printing ten pages and using none of them is no use to you.
  • 2 It does not have to be neat. Nobody else is reading it. Half sentences are fine. You are getting it out of your head and onto paper.
  • 3 Give the trackers two weeks. One entry tells you nothing. Ten start to show a pattern.
  • 4 Keep the breathing pages somewhere you can get at them. Back of a folder, in your gear bag.
This is not therapy. It does not replace your GP or a qualified mental health professional. If things are bad, tell someone and get proper support. There is a list of who to contact at mindthegael.co.uk/resources. If you feel unsafe or at risk, contact emergency services.

4-7-8 Breathing

For the night before a game, or any night your head will not switch off. Sit or lie somewhere you will not be interrupted.

Breathe in through your nose 4Seconds
Hold your breath 7Seconds
Breathe out through your mouth 8Seconds

Repeat four times

First few goes may make you light-headed. Do not do it driving, and do not do it standing in a warm-up. If you feel light-headed, shorten the counts and build up to 4-7-8.
When to use it. The night before, or on the bus. Not in the last minute before throw-in. The long out-breath settles you down, and that is not what you want two minutes before you play.

5-4-3-2-1 Grounding

For when your head has run off somewhere and you need to get back into the room. Works in a dressing room, on a sideline, or at 2am.

  • 5 Five things you can see. A cone, a boot, a mark on the wall. Anything.
  • 4 Four things you can touch. Your jersey, the bench, the ground under your feet.
  • 3 Three things you can hear. Outside your own head. Traffic, studs, someone talking.
  • 2 Two things you can smell. Grass, deep heat, your kit bag.
  • 1 One thing you can taste. Water, gum, whatever is there.
It is not supposed to feel like anything special. It gives your attention something ordinary to do instead of feeding the panic. If you get to one and still feel bad, go round again.

The S.T.O.P. Technique

For the moment you are about to react badly. Half time, a bad call, a message you are about to send.

  • S Stop. Notice that something strong is happening. Just notice it.
  • T Take a breath. One is enough. Longer out than in.
  • O Observe. Body first: heart rate, hands, jaw. Then the feeling. Then the thought underneath it.
  • P Proceed. Now decide. The same decision is fine, as long as you chose it.
Four seconds is enough. It puts a gap between what happened and how you respond.

Journal Prompts

Thirty of them. One a day, or dip in.

  1. What are you grateful for?
  2. What are you most afraid of?
  3. Where did that fear come from?
  4. What do you actually value?
  5. What matters most to you, and why?
  6. If you could do anything, what would it be?
  7. What would you go back and change?
  8. Rewrite it the way you wanted it to go.
  9. What would you change about sport if you could?
  10. Where would you go if money was no object?
  11. What would you try if you could not fail?
  12. Why are you not doing that now?
  13. Do you do things for you or to keep others happy?
  14. What goal have you set and not reached?
  15. Why did it not happen?
  16. Was that a reason or an excuse?
  17. Why these friends?
  18. What would you tell yourself at seventeen?
  19. Describe a good day, start to finish.
  20. What do you want to feel on that day?
  21. Ten things you like about yourself.
  22. Three you want to work on, and why.
  23. What makes you feel calm?
  24. Where do you want to be in five years?
  25. What are you still holding onto?
  26. Last argument: were you upset or triggered?
  27. Something you have never told anyone.
  28. What does support look like to you?
  29. What do you wish you did more of?
