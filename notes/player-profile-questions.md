# Player profile questions

Send the "To the player" bit. The rest is for you.

There are two empty slots on the Player Stories page, so two profiles fill it.

---

## How the answers turn into a page

Each profile is a tile that opens into chapters, same shape as your story in
`src/pages/about.astro`.

- **name** comes from Q1
- **blurb** you write yourself from Q2 and Q3, a line or two
- **cover** is the photo from Q12
- **chapters** come from Q4 to Q10, one chapter per answer with something in it

You write the chapter titles, not the player. Her job is the raw material.

Skip whatever she leaves blank. Three honest chapters beat six padded ones.

## To the player

Thanks for doing this.

This is for the Player Stories page on mindthegael.co.uk. The whole point is
that someone reads it and finds a bit of herself in it, and the stories that do
that are the specific ones. A real moment you remember is worth more than
anything general about pressure.

Answer as much or as little as you want, skip anything you don't fancy. Bullet
points, voice notes, one long ramble, whatever is easiest. I'll write it up and
send it back to you before it goes anywhere near the website. Nothing goes up
without you seeing it first.

Full name, first name only, or anonymous, entirely up to you.

**Basics**

1. What name do you want on it?
2. What do you play, where, and how long?
3. What position, and does that change how matches feel for you? (A keeper's
   bad day is a different thing to a corner forward's.)

**The story**

4. What was sport like for you growing up? The main thing, one of a few things,
   or something you came to later?
5. When did the mental side of it first become a problem? Not the diagnosis,
   the first time you noticed something was harder than it should have been.
6. Tell me about one specific time it got in the way. A game, a session, a team
   announcement, a conversation. What happened, what was going through your
   head, what did you do?
7. Did you ever step away, or come close to it?
8. What did you tell people at the time, and what did you keep to yourself?
9. What actually helped? Be specific. A person, a habit, a conversation, a
   professional, medication, something you do before games. Include what didn't
   help if that's part of it.
10. Where are you with it now? Honest rather than tidy. "Still working on it"
    is a real answer and probably a more useful one.

**Last two**

11. If 16 year old you read this, what would you want her to take from it?
12. Can you send a photo? One of you playing ideally, but any photo you like of
    yourself is grand. If you'd rather no photo, say so and your tile will just
    be text.

No deadline, take your time.

## For you

- Q6 carries the whole profile. If it comes back thin, go back and ask what she
  was thinking in that moment rather than asking for more detail about what
  happened.
- Q9 is what stops the page being just sad. If someone only answers the hard
  parts, ask Q9 again on its own.
- If a story sounds like something she is in the middle of right now rather
  than something she came through, the profile waits and the conversation
  matters more. Same rule as the chatbot and the plan generator.
- Check whether anyone else is identifiable before publishing. Naming a coach
  who behaved badly is a problem even when it's true.
- Get her yes in writing on the final text, not just on the answers. The
  writing up is where meaning moves.
