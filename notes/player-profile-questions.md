# Player profile questions

Questions to send to a player who has agreed to share her story for the Player
Stories page. Send the "To the player" section only. The rest is for you.

The Player Stories page has two empty slots, so two profiles fill it.

---

## How the answers map to the page

Each profile renders as a tile that opens into chapters, the same shape as
Emily's story in `src/pages/about.astro`:

| Page field  | Comes from                                            |
|-------------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| `name`      | Q1                                                    |
| `blurb`     | Written by you from Q2 and Q3, one or two lines        |
| `cover`     | The photo from Q12                                    |
| `chapters`  | Q4 to Q10, one chapter per answer that has substance   |

A chapter needs a `kicker` ("Chapter 1"), a `title` and a few paragraphs. You
write the titles, not the player. Her job is the material.

Skip any question she leaves blank rather than chasing it. A three-chapter
story that is honest beats a six-chapter one that is padded.

## To the player

Thanks for doing this. Some context on what it's for, then the questions.

This is for the Player Stories page on mindthegael.co.uk. The point of it is
that someone reading finds a bit of their own experience in yours. The stories
that do that are the specific ones, so a real moment you remember is worth more
than a general statement about pressure.

Answer as much or as little as you want. You can skip anything. Write in bullet
points, voice notes, or one long ramble, whatever's easiest, I'll shape it into
the page and send it back to you to approve before anything goes live. Nothing
goes up without you seeing it first.

You can use your full name, your first name only, or stay anonymous.

**The basics**

1. What name do you want on it? Full name, first name only, or anonymous.
2. What do you play, where, and for how long?
3. What position, and is there anything about it that shapes how you
   experience matches? (A goalkeeper's bad day is a different thing to a
   corner forward's.)

**The story**

4. What did sport look like for you growing up? Was it the main thing, one of
   several things, or something you came to later?
5. When did the mental side of it first become a problem? Not the diagnosis or
   the label, the first time you noticed something was harder than it should
   have been.
6. Describe one specific moment when it got in the way. A game, a training
   session, a team announcement, a conversation. What happened, what was going
   through your head, and what did you do?
7. Did you step away from sport at any point, or come close to it? What was
   going on, and what happened after?
8. What did you tell people at the time, and what did you keep to yourself?
9. What actually helped? Be specific. A person, a habit, a conversation, a
   professional, medication, a routine before games. Include the things that
   didn't help if that's part of it.
10. Where are you with it now? Honest rather than tidy. "Still working on it"
    is a real answer and probably a more useful one.

**Two last things**

11. If a 16 year old version of you read this, what would you want her to take
    from it?
12. Can you send a photo? Ideally one of you playing, but any photo you like of
    yourself is grand. If you'd rather have no photo, say so and your tile will
    use text only.

That's it. Take your time, there's no deadline.

## Notes for you

- Q6 is the one that carries the profile. If an answer comes back thin, that's
  the one to go back on, and the way to do it is to ask what she was thinking
  in that moment rather than asking for more detail about the event.
- Q9 is what makes the page useful rather than just sad. If someone only
  answers the hard parts, ask Q9 again on its own.
- Watch for anything that reads as a current crisis rather than a past one. If
  a story describes something she is in the middle of right now, the profile
  can wait and the conversation is the priority. Same rule as the chatbot and
  the plan generator.
- Check before publishing whether anyone else in her story is identifiable.
  Naming a coach or a selector who behaved badly is a problem even when the
  account is accurate.
- Get her approval in writing on the final text, not just on the answers. The
  shaping is where meaning shifts.
