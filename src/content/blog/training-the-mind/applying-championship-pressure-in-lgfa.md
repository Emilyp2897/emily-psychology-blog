Championship week is seven days long and most of it happens in your head.

The fitness is already in. Nothing you do this week will add to it. What is left is time, and time is the thing that does the damage.

What follows is the week as it actually runs.

## Monday: the hard session

You wake up and the match is already there, before you have opened your eyes. That is how you know it is championship week.

The hardest session of the week is Monday. In championship week it lands differently from every other Monday. It stops being a session and starts being a test, and if you're anything like me you'll have decided what a bad one means before you've finished it.

**The trap**: **"My touch was off, I'm going to play badly Saturday."**

One miss becomes a verdict.

**The reality:** one session is one session. Your brain is primed to read everything as significant this week. That is the threat state doing its job, and it is wrong.

**The reframe**: a hard session is where resilience gets built. It is not a preview of Saturday.

## Tuesday to Thursday

This is the stretch players underestimate. The hard work is behind you and the match is not here yet. You have three days to fill and Monday has just handed you the material for overthinking.

**What I'd do:**
- Treat it as a normal training week. Don't add work. The fitness is already in.
- Eat properly. Hydrate. Protect your sleep from tonight, not from Friday.
- Off social media if you can. If not, mute the accounts that make it louder.
- Have one conversation that has nothing to do with the game.

**What makes it worse:**
- Opposition video your coach did not ask you to watch.
- Extra gym sessions invented this week.
- The tactical debate in the group chat at midnight.
- Replaying Monday. It already happened and it already means nothing.

## Friday night

This is when sleep becomes the problem. You're tired. You should sleep. You can't sleep.

**What helps:**
- A wind-down routine you do every night, not just match nights.
- Phone away an hour before bed. Especially social media.
- Read something that has nothing to do with sport.
- If you're awake at 2am going over it, get up. Water, dim light, ten minutes sitting. Then back to bed. Don't lie there in the dark spinning.

You can perform on less sleep than you would like. The story you tell yourself about the lost hours does more damage than the hours.

## Saturday morning

Most players overthink the morning.

- Wake around your usual time.
- A real breakfast, three to four hours before throw-in. Carbs and protein. Nothing new on match day.
- Light movement. A walk. Some mobility.
- Let it be a normal morning.

The biggest mistake is playing the game in your head from the moment you wake. That is hours of stress banked before you have left the house. Save it for the warm-up.

## Arriving at the pitch

You see the opposition. The crowd is starting to build. Your stomach tightens.

This is where your mind makes its judgement, the fast one it makes before you have thought about anything. **"Can I handle this, or can I not?"** Everything below is about answering it the right way.

- Run your pre-match routine, the same one as any other game. See [Pre-Performance Routines](/content-hub/gael-performance-toolkit/pre-performance-routines-toolkit/).
- Don't measure yourself against the opposition warming up. They're as nervous as you are. Stay in your own bubble.
- Find your senior players. Make eye contact. Steady each other.
- Don't try to make the speech of your career in the dressing room. The squad has been preparing for months. This part is execution.

## The first five minutes

Championship games start chaotic. Both teams are full of adrenaline. Mistakes happen, to everyone, in every game.

- Give yourself one low-stakes opening action. Take an easy ball. Make a clean pass. Bank a small win.
- Nothing ambitious until you have settled.
- If your first touch goes wrong, that is information about how nervous you are. It is not information about how good you are. Reset and take the next ball.

Teams that get through the first ten minutes without a major error usually win the appraisal battle. Their bodies move from threat to challenge. Teams that make a big early mistake often spiral, because the mistake stops being a mistake and starts being a verdict about the day.

## Half-time

**What helps:**
- Hydrate. Eat if you need to.
- Sixty seconds of quiet, away from everyone.
- Two or three things from the tactical update. Not twelve.
- Reset whatever didn't work in the first half.

**What makes it worse:**
- A blame conversation, if the first half went badly.
- Getting ahead of yourself, if it went well. The game isn't over.
- Adding new tactical complexity in your head. Keep it simple.

## After the final whistle

If you won, feel it. You earned it.

If you lost, feel that too. Don't perform being fine.

Either way, the conversation in the dressing room decides how this game gets remembered. If you won, name the contributions nobody else noticed, not just the obvious heroes. If you lost, hold the room together and don't let blame start. That conversation stays with people longer than the result does.

## What to take into this championship cycle

1. **Protect your sleep all week.** Non-negotiable.
2. **Off social media.** Test it once and see.
3. **One process anchor for the game.** Not "win". Something you can actually do.
4. **Check your appraisal.** Threat or challenge? Adjust where you can.
5. **Have a teammate ally** for Friday night and Saturday morning. Steadying.

This doesn't stop you feeling it. Championship pressure is there because you care. The work is noticing what your body is doing and reading it for what it is, rather than letting it decide what the week means.
